# Woodworking planner

Tool. A web app to plan a woodworking project and see it as a 3D render, all worked out on your own computer, before you cut the first board.

In development: the first version works locally, in the Workbench layout (direction A in `design/DESIGN.md`).

## What it's for

For anyone building furniture or other projects from boards and sheet goods: lay the parts out in 3D, catch mistakes such as boards that overlap, and get the cut list before buying wood or cutting anything. Nothing leaves your computer: no account and no server.

## First version

- A project is a name and a list of parts. Each part has a shape, its sizes in millimetres, the position of its left, bottom, back corner, which way it faces (one of the 24 ways a piece can point), and its wood.
- Six shapes: Board (length, width, thickness), Disc (diameter, thickness), Dowel (diameter, length), Arc (a ring segment: outer radius, band width, sweep in degrees, thickness), Triangle (right-angled: length and width are the two legs, plus thickness) and Free (an outline of 3 to 64 points, each edge straight or bent into one circular arc, plus thickness). Switching shape keeps length, width and thickness, and a free shape keeps its outline while it is another shape, so switching back loses nothing. Switching to Free turns the piece's outline into points (a disc becomes four points with bends, rounded corners become bent edges) without changing its size, or brings back the outline it had as a free shape, stretched if its length or width changed meanwhile. A full 360° ring that never was a free shape is refused, since a free shape has no hole, and so is a piece too thin for its points to stay 1 mm apart. A free shape's length and width are its blank, measured round its bent edges too; changing one stretches the outline that way and keeps the other.
- The outline editor (Edit outline… in the edit panel, and opened straight away when Free adds a piece) edits a free shape in a drawing and a table of points at once. Drag a point (on the 10 mm grid with Snap on, Alt for the opposite), drag the diamond in the middle of an edge to bend it into an arc, double-click an edge to add a point there, or type From left, From bottom and Bend; arrow keys move the picked point and Page Up/Down pick another. Draw turns one freehand stroke into at most 24 points, with arcs where it curves. It has its own undo, works with one finger on a phone (full screen, with the dragged point's position shown), and refuses Done with a spoken reason while the outline crosses itself, has two points together, has no area or is over 10,000 mm across. Done is one undo step and keeps the piece's left, bottom, back corner where it was; Cancel with changes asks before discarding them.
- A board's corners can be square, rounded or cut: pick which of the four corners, then one radius, or for a cut how far it runs along the length and along the width. One treatment per board.
- Add, duplicate, delete and edit parts. Six equal buttons under Add a piece (Board, Disc, Dowel, Arc, Triangle, Free; on a phone in the bar at the bottom) each add that shape in one press. A copy goes on the floor beside the original, past any part already there. Clicking a part in the 3D view picks it in the list and the other way round. The list shows each part's outline in its wood colour.
- Move a board by dragging it in the 3D view once it is picked. Floor or Height in the toolbar picks whether it slides along the floor or goes up and down; holding Shift switches mid-drag and letting go switches back, so one drag can slide, lift and slide again. With Snap on (remembered per browser) it sits flush where it would meet another piece along the way it is dragged, by the true outlines (on a curve, the first point where they touch), lines up with the faces of pieces out of its way, or sits on a 10 mm grid. Any piece of a dragged assembly can be the one that sits flush, an open door where it stands, and lifting stops the assembly's lowest piece on the floor. Holding Alt does the opposite, also mid-drag. Arrow keys nudge it 10 mm, Shift+arrow 1 mm, Alt+↑/↓ back and forth. A whole drag is one undo step.
- Join two pieces that touch with screws, nails, pocket screws, glue, an angle bracket, a mending plate, a corner plate, a cut joint or a hinge (Joints in the edit panel; the Join with list groups them as Fixings, Plates and brackets, Cut joints and Moving; the To list offers only pieces whose true outlines meet on a face, so a disc in the corner of a board's box is not offered). One joint per pair; screws, nails, pocket screws, brackets and plates can also be glued. A mending plate is a flat plate across a butt joint on a face that runs flush across both pieces (by default the widest such face a stock plate fits, so two thin panels lying flat take it on their broad face); a corner plate is a flat L on such a face where the pieces meet in an L or a T, and is refused for pieces in line. Pocket screws go from the end or edge of the picked piece into the other (if the picked piece meets it with its face, the other one is drilled), with the pockets on one of its broad faces (by default the one facing the other piece, so a top on two sides gets them inside both, or, when the other piece runs past both faces or neither, whichever of its back, bottom or left faces is a broad one); two pieces face to face are refused. Joined pieces form an assembly: dragging, nudging, typing a position or Turn, Tip and Roll moves the whole assembly, and Delete removes the piece's joints with it.
- A hinged piece swings on one edge of the face it shares with its frame (Left, Right, Top, Bottom, Back or Front edge; the edge nearest the frame by default), away from the frame. Dragging it in the 3D view swings it to any angle up to its limit (5° steps with Snap, catching 0°, 90° and the limit); dragging the frame moves both. Open angle, a slider, Close and Open fully sit in the joint. Positions are kept as if every hinge were closed, but snapping and the ±10,000 mm position limit go by where an open door actually stands. A piece swings on only one hinge, nothing can be joined to a piece while its hinge is open, and a hinge whose door is also fixed to its frame some other way can't swing and says why. A door stops at the first piece in its way (every piece but the one it hangs on and those fixed to the door), tested with the swung piece's true turned box and rounded down to a whole degree so it rests just short of touching (a piece the closed door touches or almost touches may be grazed by up to 1 mm, so an inset door snapped flush between two sides still opens): dragging, the slider, typing an angle and Open fully all stop there, and the card, its summary and the 3D chip say what stopped it (`Stopped by Shelf 2 at 74°`), with that piece outlined in the accent colour while the door rests there. Unticking Stops at the first piece in its way, per hinge, lets it swing through as before, and any piece it passes through is flagged as overlapping, using the swung piece's true outline at its angle. A door already open past a piece is left where it is and warned about.
- Each joint has sizes worked out from the pieces it joins, which can be changed and reset with Use suggested sizes: screws and nails (count, diameter, length, about 2.5 times the piece they go through, never longer than both pieces), angle brackets (count, leg, width, screws for each bracket sized from the thinner piece, as short as 6 on stock under 12 thick so they stay inside it; a bracket needs an inside corner), mending and corner plates (stock sizes up to 80 × 16 that fit the pieces, refused when not even the smallest fits, two mending plates on a joint line over 300, four screws each the longest of 12, 16 or 20 at least 2 shorter than the thinner piece, or 6 to 10 on stock under 12 thick, and the face they sit on), pocket screws (two plus one per 150 of joint line over 150, ⌀4, 25 long in pieces under 15 thick, 32 up to 22, 38 up to 30, 64 above) and hinges (butt, concealed with a 35 mm cup, or piano; count by door height, length, screws each and their size, how far it opens). Warnings flag a joint whose pieces no longer touch, a screw longer than both pieces, plate and bracket screws longer than a piece is thick, a plate wider than the face it sits on or longer than the pieces leave room for, pocket screws that come out the far side of the other piece, and concealed hinges on a door under 16 mm thick.
- The 3D view draws the hardware: hinge knuckles on the hinge line (a concealed hinge has a short one on the frame beside each cup), screw and nail heads on the outside face, brackets in the inside corner, plates with their screw heads on their face across the joint (the L of a corner plate in the inside corner), pocket holes as dark ovals on the drilled piece by its end, and the picked door's hinge line in the accent colour.
- Cut joints (Join with, under Cut joints): a dado, a mortise and tenon, or dowels. Pieces stay placed flush, as they look assembled, and the joint adds the hidden part: the piece that meets the other with its end (for a dado, its end or edge) goes in, into a face of the other (a mortise can also be in its edge; end to end and edge into end grain are refused), and is cut longer by the tenon length or the dado depth at that end (wider, for a dado along its edge). Sizes are suggested from the pieces (dado a third of the housing piece's thickness; tenon two thirds of the mortised piece's depth where the tenon sits, rounded down to 5, a third of the rail's thickness and its width less 10 at each edge; dowels by the thinner piece, ⌀6, ⌀8 or ⌀10, one per 50 mm of joint) and can be changed or reset; a tenon can go right through. Only a board goes in: a piece switched to another shape keeps the joint flagged, and it adds no length and draws no tenon. Sizes the pieces can't take are refused naming the piece, and a dado deeper than half its piece, a tenon thicker than half its rail and dowels longer than their two holes are flagged. The 3D view draws the tenon or housed end in the piece's own wood, so only what really shows is seen (a through tenon's end, a dado at the front edge), and traces the tenon, the groove and the dowels in the accent colour through the wood while either piece is picked.
- Hardware list under the cut list: hinges, brackets, mending and corner plates, screws (including those for brackets, plates and hinges), pocket screws, nails and dowel pins, grouped by size with where they are used, plus glued joints, and a hardware count in the totals.
- Lock a board in place (edit panel or its row in the list): a locked board can't be dragged, nudged, rotated, resized or deleted, and a drag that starts on it turns the view instead. A locked piece pins every piece joined to it (position, Lies and turning are off for all of them), but a door hinged on it can still swing. A locked door stays at its angle, and so does an open door with a locked piece fixed to it: changing how far it opens, the hinge type or edge, or Use suggested sizes is refused when it would swing it, and so are resizing or reshaping the piece it hangs on, removing its hinge and deleting that piece. Remove joint and Delete look off, and pressing them (or the Delete key) says which locked piece is in the way, as does the field you changed. Its name and wood can still change, the lock is saved with the project, and a copy of it starts unlocked.
- Turn, Tip and Roll rotate the picked piece 90° (keys R, T, L), so a triangle, an arc or a board with some corners shaped can face any of the 24 ways. It turns about the middle of its footprint and keeps its underside where it was, so a piece on the floor or on another piece stays resting there, however often it is turned. Two turns about the same axis face it the other way; four bring it back. The Lies list sets the six main ways directly, for the picked piece only: on a joined piece it can pull that piece off its joints, while Turn, Tip and Roll turn the whole assembly; any other way shows as the nearest of the six plus how it is turned (for example `Lying flat, length front–back, spun half a turn`).
- The 3D view redraws only when something changes, so an idle page leaves the GPU alone.
- Potato (top bar, remembered per browser) draws faster on slow computers: 1× pixels, no shadows, flat colours without grain and simpler lighting. Boards keep their wood colour, which costs nothing. On a software renderer at 2× pixel density, turning the view went from 18–29 to 60 frames a second.
- Debug (top bar) shows the graphics card in use, frames per second, GPU time per frame where the browser allows measuring it, and the app's CPU time. A web page cannot read the whole computer's GPU or CPU load, so these are the app's own share.
- 3D view with wood colours and grain along the length, shadows, a 100 mm floor grid, orbit, zoom and pan, Iso, Front, Side and Top cameras, and Fit.
- Workbench layout: parts on the left, the 3D view with the cut list under it in the middle, the selected board on the right. On a phone the 3D view stays on top with Parts, Edit and Cut list tabs below.
- Parts that overlap are flagged in the list, by their true solid rather than their box: rounded and cut corners, discs, round dowels, the bend of an arc, the slope of a triangle and the straight and bent edges of a free shape, in any of the 24 poses and on a swung piece at any angle. Curves are checked as polygons within 0.4 mm of the true curve that never reach past it, so pieces that only touch are never flagged, and a piece in the empty corner of another's box neither overlaps nor touches it.
- Cut list: identical parts of the same wood and shape are grouped. A shaped part lists the rectangular blank to cut it from, with its shape named under it (for example `Disc ⌀400`, `2 corners cut 50 × 80` or `Free shape, 7 points`); a dowel lists the rod, `⌀30 × 450`. A piece in a cut joint lists its cut size with what that includes under it (`Length includes 2 × 30 tenons`), and the piece that is cut says so (`2 mortises 30 deep`, `Dado 18 × 6`, `4 dowel holes ⌀8`); pieces share a row only when those match too, and the edit panel's Cut from line says the same. Totals give the part count, volume, face area and approximate weight from the true shape plus what cut joints add (mortises, grooves and holes are not taken off), plus the overall size.
- Cutting plan: a List / Plan switch in the Cut list pane (remembered per browser) lays each piece's rectangular blank, at its cut-list size with tenons and dados included, onto stock of the same wood and thickness: sheets for birch plywood and MDF, boards for the other woods. A free shape's blank is the box round its outline. Saw kerf (default 3 mm) is left between pieces and edge trim (default 0) comes off every edge first; both are saved with the project. Each wood and thickness picks a stock size from presets (sheets 2,440 × 1,220 by default; boards default to the smallest preset that fits the most pieces, with glued panels as well) or its own size. Each piece keeps its grain along the stock's length unless Keep the grain along the length is unticked (unticked by default for MDF), which lets the plan turn it a quarter turn. The plan says how many sheets and boards to buy and the waste, and draws each sheet or board to scale with its pieces, kerf, trim and offcuts worth keeping (both sides at least 100 mm); identical layouts are drawn once. Pressing a piece in a drawing or its key button picks it. A piece too big for its stock is named with what to do and flagged in the Parts list. Dowels are listed as rod, not laid out. The same input always gives the same layout.
- Undo and redo (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z). Rapid typing in one field counts as one step; each tick, choice or list pick is a step of its own.
- Autosaves in the browser. Save and open a project as a `.woodplan.json` file. Files are format version 6, which adds the cutting plan's saw kerf, edge trim and stock sizes and each piece's grain tick, mending plates, corner plates, pocket screws and the door stop (a hinge saved without it has the stop on), all optional. Version 5 added the cut joints (`dado`, `tenon` and `dowels`, optional, so a file without them opens with none), and version 4 the outline of a free shape (`points`, saved on any piece that has been a free shape, so a board, disc or arc that was one gets its outline back on switching to Free; a free shape saved without it opens as its blank rectangle). Version 1 to 5 files and autosaves still open with the cutting plan's defaults and each wood's grain tick, version 1 and 2 with no joints, and version 1 parts as boards with square corners lying exactly as they did.
- Opens with an example bookshelf; New starts an empty project and Example brings the bookshelf back.

## Run it

```bash
npm start
```

Then open http://127.0.0.1:8777/. There is no build step. The app lives in `docs/app/`, which maksforge.com serves at `/woodworking-planner/app/`; the rest of `docs/` is its page there. three.js is vendored in `docs/app/vendor/three/` (0.180.0, MIT), with one change: `addons/OrbitControls.js` imports `../three.module.js` instead of the bare name `three`, because the site's Content-Security-Policy blocks an inline import map.

A service worker (`docs/app/sw.js`) keeps a copy of every app file so it works offline after the first visit, and `manifest.webmanifest` makes it installable. `tests/offline.test.js` fails when a file is added without being listed in `sw.js`; bump its `CACHE` name whenever the app changes. The worker fetches from the network first, so visitors online already get new files; the bump refreshes the offline copy and clears files that were removed.
